# Want to (たい)

Ichidan verbs drop る, godan verbs take their i-sound, + たい. 食[た]べる → 食[た]べたい, 書[か]く → 書[か]きたい. It then conjugates like an い-adjective: 食[た]べたくない (don't want to), 食[た]べたかった (wanted to).

#### Meaning

What **you** want to do (or, in a question, what the listener wants to do):

- 日本[にほん]に **行[い]きたい**: I want to go to Japan.
- 何[なに]が **食[た]べたい**?: What do you want to eat?

Not used for what someone else wants: for that, Japanese says 食[た]べたがっている (seems to want to eat).

#### Tone

**Plain** (casual): used with close friends and family, or with people younger than you. Add です to make it polite: 行[い]きたいです.
